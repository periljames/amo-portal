## Architecture and sources of truth

Phase 8 reuses \`QMSNotification\`, \`external_partner_notifications\`, existing \`email_logs\`/Resend delivery events, and the \`portal_scheduler_main\` scheduled process. The \`external_provider_alert_jobs\` table is a **durable reconciliation/delivery ledger**, not a competing user-facing notification center. It has a unique tenant/fingerprint constraint, retry state, attempts, cancellation, delivery and acknowledgement timestamps. \`external_provider_alert_policies\` and \`external_provider_alert_preferences\` hold tenant departmental routing/dates/channels and individually scoped partner-member choices. Three tables are protected by PostgreSQL FORCE RLS and tenant/provider constraints. New Alembic revision: \`extprov_261018_alerts\` with parent \`extprov_261017_main_merge\`.

The scheduled scanner reads immutable Phase 4 qualification/restriction and Phase 6–7 Procurement/contracted-work transitions, supported certificate/evidence/approval/contract dates, recurring surveillance dates, canonical RFQ response deadlines, PO acknowledgement and promised-delivery dates, and manager-authored external work ETA/acknowledgement deadlines. It never treats a frontend badge as regulatory approval and never updates supplier or technical eligibility. Source status/date is rechecked when a reminder dispatches; superseded due dates, closed contracts and completed acknowledgements cancel pending obligations instead of generating stale notices. Phase 7 new dates and subsequent edits require the responsible Maintenance Manager and are recorded with attributable event provenance; contractor ETA proposals do not automatically amend authorized work dates.

Default **example** lead days: 120, 90, 60, 30, 14 and 7. They are **tenant-editable policy settings**, not statutory requirements. Default overdue escalation offsets: 0, 3 and 7 days. Scheduling uses the AMO tenant timezone, handles date-based milestones and allows quiet hours and daily email delivery windows. State-change notifications use stable authoritative event identity and do not repeat each worker scan; date-based alerts include current revision and each separate milestone in their identity. Work-item notifications deep-link to the exact authorized supplier/work action; server permission checks remain authoritative after link navigation.

Quality, Procurement, Maintenance and Stores have department-owned settings and dispatch history in their respective workspaces. Administrators have controlled tenant-wide delivery windows. External provider members can manage only their own notification preferences from the partner activity page; assigned contracted-work links and acknowledgements require current tenant/member/object grant checks. Provider email **attempts** are now opt-in only, require an ACTIVE tenant/provider grant and a verified external identity address, and use the existing Resend-backed `send_email` service. Staff email is also optional. An API hand-off is never proof of email inbox delivery or reading; signed webhook state remains authoritative. The ledger's \`DELIVERED\` state represents projection to the selected notification channel, **not** confirmed email inbox delivery or read. Retries/backoff and retry error visibility are available in the dispatcher and departmental UI.

## Security / trust boundaries

- No supplier approval, release, inspection signoff, written technical authorization, new contracted work, supplier eligibility or mandatory Quality control is granted by a notification.
- Portal and Quality roles are scoped to the tenant and owned department; vendor notifications are scoped to an active individual grant and specific assigned work.
- Generic messages omit technical/evidence contents, contract details, object store URLs and third-party addresses. Custom labels are limited plain text; never interpolate untrusted provider payloads.
- The delivery worker refreshes membership, tenant, role and current source state before dispatch. Duplicate delivery to a single recipient is prevented by the tenant/fingerprint uniqueness constraint, not an in-memory cache.
- Delivery retry/digest/quiet rules do not supersede official in-app evidence of technical/Quality decisions, and email HTTP 200/202 is never interpreted as recipient delivery or read.

## 19 October 2026 — Phase 8 same-PR continuation and source-level repairs

The review of existing Phase 7 and Phase 8 source found misleading event names: an accepted work assignment was described as a new assignment; a normal progress note or proposed ETA was described as an overdue deadline; a Quality escalation was described as a suspension; and a completed PO acknowledgement was described as outstanding. These mappings now preserve the exact nature of the authoritative event. A contract, supplier or maintenance release cannot be altered by notification projection.

- Canonical Procurement event projection now reads existing internal `procurement_events` (PO sending/acknowledgements, receiving to quarantine, inspection disposition, Quality holds and release) in addition to external Procurement events. External partner site/capability proposals, evidence review states and contract receipt/clarification records also feed the existing tenant center. The status of a rejected or unknown inspection is never reported as accepted; receiving to quarantine is not stock release.
- Manager-controlled work acknowledgement and completion reminders are cancelled when the current work state or dates no longer qualify. Same-day expiry notices never declare an evening deadline missed in the morning. Current tenant policy offsets and contract notice days are rechecked before dispatch; previous reminders are superseded. RFQ deadline scanning is limited to issued/responding RFQs with unanswered invitations. A bounded seven-day reminder catch-up reduces brief scheduler outage gaps.
- PostgreSQL provider FORCE RLS now scopes recipient alert rows **and personal preferences** to the exact provider identity in addition to provider and tenant, and blocks external sessions from tenant-wide policies. Provider dispatch checks active identity, membership, grant, exact assigned work, portal state and opted-in verified email, while notifications about existing authorized work remain possible when only **new work** is restricted.
- Email logs carry separate provider acceptance and verified delivery status. Departmental dispatch history displays transport status from `email_logs`, not a fabricated delivered/read flag. Skipped user-preference email is not counted as a delivered email. Retry correlation keys include the attempt to permit retry after a genuinely failed send while still deduplicating same-attempt submission.
- Maintenance and Stores receive owned settings controls alongside existing Quality/Procurement controls. Canonical Procurement notices link to individual authorized RFQ/PO/receipt/hold records (the existing API remains the authorization boundary). Partner work-alert links are resolved through an authenticated, member-scoped action endpoint.

**Scope limitation:** Phase 8 now projects additional verified canonical records but does not claim every originally requested event is fully connected. In particular, existing QMS audit/CAR/CAPA notification ownership, all contract signature/renewal edge cases, Stores return/RMA variants and complete technical release/inspection closure matrices still require source-to-event trace and Phase 10 runtime acceptance. Scheduler crash/retry exactly-once semantics, queue retention/performance, migration DAG, recipient revocation, email delivery webhooks, timezone/DST and actual browser deep links have not been exercised. The Phase 10 verification ledger makes these explicit blockers. This is source implementation, **not** production readiness or compliance certification. No tests, builds, lint, CI, or database migrations were intentionally executed; no Phase 9 or merge.
